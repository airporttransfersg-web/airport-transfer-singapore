## What is a Changi Airport transfer?

A Changi Airport transfer is a pre-booked private hire car assigned to one flight and one destination, at a fare fixed before travel. It is not a shared shuttle sold per seat, and it is not a car found in an app after you clear customs. The booking exists before you land, which is what allows the driver to be inside the terminal when you walk out.

Arriving travellers describe it in several ways depending on where they have flown from. Changi airport pickup and Changi airport drop off are the most common searches from within Asia; British and Australian passengers tend to say airport transfer; Gulf and Chinese travellers more often ask for a Changi airport chauffeur or a limousine transfer and expect an executive MPV. Changi's own premium arrival services, such as the private terminal handling at JetQuay, are a separate product that sits alongside the transfer rather than replacing it.

Compared with the taxi rank, a pre-booked transfer trades a metered fare for a known one and trades a queue for a name board. Compared with Grab, it trades app convenience for a driver who is already at the terminal when the aircraft lands, on a fare that does not respond to how many people landed with you. That difference is smallest at 11:00 on a Tuesday and largest during the late-evening arrival bank, when three or four wide-bodies land inside an hour and the pickup bays back up.

#### Waiting time, midnight and holiday charges at Changi

Waiting is free for 60 minutes from touchdown, measured from the aircraft's landed time rather than the scheduled arrival. That window is set to cover immigration and baggage on a full flight, including the Terminal 3 evening bank. After it, additional waiting starts from S$20 per 15 minutes and may vary depending on the vehicle type. After three blocks it converts to the vehicle's hourly rate.

Departures from a Singapore address carry a 15-minute grace period from the booked time. Pickups between 23:00 and 06:59 carry a late-night loading of S$20 per transfer, and S$30 on the 40-seater coach. Trips out to Tuas or Kranji carry S$30. A terminal change after booking costs nothing.

#### Add-ons at Changi: extra stops, child seats and the VIP terminal

**Extra stop:** S$20, one stop only, on the way, with 15 minutes of waiting included. A second stop means an hourly booking instead.

**Child seats:** S$20 per seat per trip. A capsule for under 9 months, which fits the Business Van only; an Urban Kanga from 9 months to 3 years; or a Mifold from 3 to 5 years. Book with the child's age.

**Porter service:** baggage assistance from the carousel to the vehicle, arranged with the terminal on request.

**VIP private terminal:** Changi's JetQuay handling, at S$600 per passenger, with S$120 added for Terminal 4 and a 25 per cent surcharge between 23:00 and 07:00. It covers a greeting at the aircraft, a private immigration counter, priority customs, lounge access and a handover straight to your driver. It is arranged alongside the transfer, not instead of it.

## Who books Changi transfers

Different passengers fail in different places at Changi, and the booking is really a fix for that failure.

### Long-haul arrivals landing after midnight

The late bank into T1 and T3 lands when ride-hailing supply is at its thinnest and the taxi queue is at its longest. A pre-booked car is already parked when the aircraft touches down, and the fare was agreed the week before, when nobody was bidding against you.

### Executive assistants meeting inbound directors

The executive assistant needs proof the car exists before the director lands. Driver name and mobile number go out the evening before, the flight is tracked without anyone asking, and the trip appears on a monthly invoice with a cost-centre reference instead of on someone's personal card.

### Families with children and a full luggage load

Four cases, a pram and two tired children do not fit into an Executive MPV or into a 25-minute wait at the pickup bay. Child seats are fitted before the vehicle leaves the depot, and the vehicle is sized against the declared bag count rather than the headcount.

### Cruise passengers connecting from a flight

An arrival that has to reach Marina Bay Cruise Centre before boarding closes has a real deadline. The pickup is planned backwards from the terminal cut-off, and a delayed inbound flight triggers a call rather than a silent wait.

### Airshow and conference delegations

Changi Exhibition Centre during the Singapore Airshow, and the Expo during Fintech Festival week, both concentrate thousands of arrivals into a few days. Vehicles are blocked out against the dates well before the manifest is final.

## Mistakes people make booking Changi transfers

1. **Booking against the scheduled arrival time instead of the flight number.** Without the flight number the driver is working to a clock, and a two-hour delay becomes a waiting charge.
2. **Wandering into Jewel after clearing customs.** The driver is at the arrival exit, and Jewel is a different building.
3. **Sizing the car by seats.** Four passengers with four checked bags need an Executive MPV at least, and finding that out at the kerb costs time and money.
4. **Timing a departure from the flight time.** Check-in closes an hour before, and Changi's approach roads are busiest during the evening departure bank.
5. **Accepting a car offered to you inside the terminal.** Unlicensed touting exists at every large airport and you have no insurance cover as a passenger.
6. **Assuming a T3 booking covers a T4 arrival.** The terminals are a drive apart, not a walk.
7. **Booking three separate transfers for one working day.** From three stops onward, an hourly booking is cheaper and keeps the same driver.
8. **Leaving a child seat request to the arrivals hall.** It has to be fitted before the car leaves.
