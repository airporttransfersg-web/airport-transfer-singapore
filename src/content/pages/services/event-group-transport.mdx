## What is event and group transport?

Event and group transport is a coordinated set of vehicle movements planned against a schedule rather than around individual passengers. A group transfer is one leg with several vehicles departing together. An event programme is a series of those legs across a day or several days, usually with vehicles standing by between them.

The wording varies by who is buying. Conference organisers ask for delegate transport or shuttle services. Wedding planners ask for bridal cars and guest transfers. Corporate event teams ask for VIP transport or speaker transport. Sports and entertainment organisers ask for crew and talent movements. The underlying arrangement is the same: vehicles allocated to a schedule, with a coordinator between the organiser and the drivers.

The difference from booking several [Changi Airport transfers](/services/changi-airport-transfers/) separately is accountability. Four independent bookings produce four drivers who do not know about each other, arriving at whichever door their map suggests. A group booking produces one plan, briefed once, with someone whose job is to answer the phone when the schedule slips. On event days in Singapore the schedule always slips, usually because a session overruns rather than because of the road.

## Who books group transport

Conference and exhibition organisers. Delegates arrive on 20 different flights and have to reach a venue that is expecting all of them at 09:00. Arrivals are staged against the actual flight schedule, with a vehicle plan that thins out as the day goes on rather than a fleet sitting idle at Changi from dawn.

Corporate event and incentive teams. The programme is fixed months ahead, the manifest is not. Vehicles are blocked out on the dates and the passenger lists land days before, without repricing.

Wedding planners and families. The bridal car has to arrive at a specific minute and guests have to be moved from hotels in three different districts. Guest vehicles run to a timetable and the bridal vehicle is dedicated, not shared with the shuttle.

Sports, entertainment and production. Grand Prix weekend closes roads around Marina Bay for days, which changes every route into the district. Crews move to call times, talent moves to a separate plan, and both need drivers who know the accredited access points.

Delegations and trade missions. Fifteen to forty people arriving together, a fixed programme of site visits, and a protocol requirement about who travels in which vehicle. Convoys move together with one coordinator.

Schools, teams and tour groups. Simpler in shape, heavier on luggage, and usually needing more vehicles than the headcount suggests once sports gear or instruments are counted.

## How many vehicles does a group need?

Count the bags before the people. A group of 12 with cabin bags fits one Minibus; the same 12 off a long-haul flight with checked luggage needs two.

Minibuses beat a single coach in Singapore more often than organisers expect: hotel porches and event drop-offs are sized for cars, and several vehicles unload in parallel while a coach unloads in sequence from a bay further away.

## Venue access and drop-off points

Marina Bay Sands. Separate drop-offs for the hotel towers, the Sands Expo convention level and the shopping galleria. Event traffic is directed by venue staff, and the tower lobby drop-off is not always open during large functions. Name the tower.

Suntec Convention Centre. Vehicle access is via the basement drop-off, with the exhibition halls a level above. During large exhibitions the queue on Temasek Boulevard is the delay, not the drive.

Singapore Expo. Multiple halls with separate entrances, spread over a long frontage. Give the hall number; walking between halls with luggage takes 10 minutes.

Resorts World Sentosa. Island access is controlled and drop-off points differ by hotel. Vehicles pay the island entry charge, which is inside the quoted fare.

Marina Bay Street Circuit, Grand Prix weekend. Road closures start days before the race and change access to Marina Bay, Raffles Place and the Fullerton area. Drop-offs move to accredited points, and the practical answer is often a drop two streets out with a short walk.

Changi Exhibition Centre, Airshow. A dedicated access road with event traffic control and staged entry times. Shuttle timetables are set with the organiser rather than by us.

Hotels on Orchard Road. Kerbside stopping is restricted along most of it, and porches queue during check-in hours. A group of vehicles arrives in sequence rather than together.

Two weeks is the working minimum for a multi-vehicle day. These weeks need more, because the whole island's fleet is committed at once.

Where Minibuses lose: for 40 people moving from one hotel to one venue and back, the coach is cheaper per head and we will tell you so. Minibuses win when the group splits, when luggage is heavy, or when the drop-off point cannot take a coach.

## Mistakes organisers make

1. Sizing vehicles from the headcount without counting bags. It is the single most common cause of an extra vehicle on the day, at the worst price.
2. Giving one arrival time for a group on 20 different flights. Staging arrivals costs less and leaves nobody waiting two hours at Changi.
3. Booking to the venue name rather than the hall or tower. Singapore Expo and Marina Bay Sands are both large enough for that to cost 10 minutes and a lot of goodwill.
4. Planning Grand Prix week routes on a normal map. Closures start days before the race.
5. Releasing vehicles between morning and evening movements. Standby costs less than rebooking twice, and rebooking may not be possible on an event day.
6. Leaving the manifest open until the morning. Vehicle counts can flex; driver allocation cannot flex at 06:00.
7. Treating the bridal car as part of the guest shuttle. It needs to be dedicated and early.
8. Assuming a coach can reach every hotel porch. Many cannot, and the group ends up walking with luggage.
