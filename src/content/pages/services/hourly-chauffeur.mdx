## What is an hourly chauffeur service?

An hourly chauffeur service is the hire of a vehicle with a professional driver for a continuous period, billed by the hour, where waiting time is part of what you are paying for rather than a penalty. The driver stays with the vehicle between stops, the luggage and the laptop bag stay in the car, and the itinerary can change without a new booking.

The term is used differently around the world, which is why the same request arrives in several forms. In the UK and Australia it is chauffeur hire or a car with driver. In the United States it is an as-directed booking or an hourly car service. Across the Gulf and Greater China, limousine service usually describes the same arrangement with an expectation of an executive MPV. Singapore's own regulatory language is plainer: a private hire car with a licensed driver, booked in advance.

The difference from ride-hailing is not the vehicle, it is the commitment. A Grab or Gojek trip ends when you get out, and the next one starts with a fresh search, a fresh price and a driver who has never met you. An hourly booking holds one car for the whole window, which is what makes a nine-stop property viewing day or a five-meeting roadshow possible at all.

#### Overtime, late nights and cross-border

Overtime runs in 30-minute increments at the booked hourly rate. The driver flags when the block is close to its end; you authorise the extension, operations confirms it, and you are not billed for part-increments.

Hours worked between 23:00 and 06:59 carry a late-night loading of S$20 per hour, and S$35 on the 40-seater coach. Work out at Tuas or Kranji carries S$30 per trip.

Crossing into Malaysia adds a cross-border surcharge, quoted with the trip and requires notice, because not every vehicle is permitted and the driver needs the paperwork. Queue time at Woodlands or Tuas counts inside the block.

## What to know before an hourly booking

When does the clock start?
At the booked start time at your first pickup, not when the driver leaves the depot. It stops when you release the driver at the final stop.

Does waiting count as driving time?
Yes. Waiting is the point of an hourly booking, and it is charged at the same rate as moving.

Where does the driver wait?
In the vehicle where parking allows, and in the nearest legal holding area where it does not. Raffles Place, Marina Bay Financial Centre and Orchard Road all have restricted kerbside access, so the driver may be two minutes away rather than outside the door. Call five minutes before you come down.

Are tolls, ERP and parking included?
Yes, all of them, and Singapore's ERP charges vary by corridor and time. A day that crosses the CBD repeatedly costs no more.

Can the block start at the airport?
Yes. A common shape is a Changi pickup that rolls straight into a working day, booked as one hourly block rather than a transfer plus a booking.

Can I change the itinerary during the day?
Yes, within Singapore and within the block. Tell the driver; nothing needs to go through operations.

Do you cover Malaysia?
Yes, with notice and a surcharge. The checkpoint queue is inside your block, and on a Friday evening or a public holiday eve it can be an hour.

Peak windows to plan around?
07:30 to 09:30 and 17:30 to 19:30 on weekdays. A 17:00 meeting in Marina Bay that ends at 18:15 will take 25 minutes to leave the district.

## Mistakes people make with hourly bookings

1. Booking the minimum block for a day that will obviously run longer. Overtime is fine, but a 4-hour block for a 7-hour day means the driver may have a commitment after yours.
2. Booking legs instead of hours for a multi-stop day. Four separate transfers cost more, involve four drivers, and your bags travel with you every time.
3. Giving the office address rather than the tower and lobby. Marina Bay Financial Centre has three towers and Suntec has five.
4. Expecting the car to be parked outside the door in the CBD. Kerbside stopping is restricted through most of it.
5. Not declaring luggage on an airport-to-meetings block. The bags stay in the car all day, so the boot has to be big enough from the start.
6. Assuming the driver can cross into Malaysia on the day. Vehicle permits and notice are needed.
7. Releasing the driver early and expecting a refund. The block is committed at booking; if the day ends early, use the time.
8. Booking a Business Van for eleven people. Seven is seven; that group needs a Minibus, and above thirteen it needs two vehicles.
